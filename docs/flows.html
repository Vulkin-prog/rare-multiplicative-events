A strict integer record requires a longer run. Equal lengths retain the earliest holder. A record is counted when its visible length first exceeds the previous maximum.

Shared arithmetic setting and hypotheses

The underlying function is completely multiplicative, with independent fair signs at primes. Set

\beta=\log 2,\quad L=\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor-a_M,\quad a_M\in\mathbb N_0,\quad a_M=o(\log M),\quad n=M-L,\quad\Lambda=n2^{-L}.

Condition on a positive-probability event A_M\in\mathcal F_{Y_M^\sharp} with information cost I_M=-\log\mathbb P(A_M). Taking the whole probability space gives unconditional statements. The hard scales of §2.2 are

H=\log M,\quad\nu_M=H/V_M,\quad V_M=D(H/V_M),\quad Y_M^\sharp=\lfloor e^{V_M}\rfloor,
D(\nu)=e^{u(\nu)}-\operatorname{Ei}(u(\nu)),\qquad e^{u(\nu)}/u(\nu)=\nu,\quad u(\nu)>1.

Use the large solution specified in the manuscript; V_M\sim\sqrt{\tfrac12H\log H}. For a fixed c>0, the information–intensity budget is

\Lambda\ge1,\qquad I_M+\log\Lambda\le V_M-c\nu_M.

Choose T=\log_2(n/m_0),\ h=T/J\le1. The first cell \mathcal A_0=\{1,\ldots,m_0\} retains the earlier history; the remaining cells have endpoints m_r=\lfloor m_0 2^{rh}\rfloor\ (r<J),\ m_J=n. Require

T=O(V_M),\qquad\log(2+T+h^{-1})=o(\nu_M),\qquad m_0h\to\infty.

For growing record and scale histories, add

T\to\infty,\qquad\Lambda2^{-T}\to\infty,\qquad Th\to0.

The observation window is late, beginning at M^{1-o(1)}; the historical maximum and holder are not reset at its beginning. These are sufficient proof ranges, not asserted optimal boundaries. §§2.1–2.5, pp. 7–10 ↗

01Input 2.1 · Corollary 2.2 ↗

One complete field comparison, every retained observation

Use L=\lfloor\log_2M\rfloor-a_M,\quad a_M\in\mathbb N_0,\quad a_M=o(\log M),\quad n=M-L,\quad\Lambda=n2^{-L}\to\infty. For a positive-probability event A_M\in\mathcal F_{Y_M^\sharp}, let \mathcal I_M=-\log\mathbb P(A_M). The site index ranges over 1\le j\le n. Under the hard one-factor budget

\mathcal I_M+\log\Lambda\le V_M-c\nu_M\qquad(c>0),

the field I of all starts j+1, exact full excess e≥0 and actual signs σ is compared to independent Poisson coordinates P of mean 2−L−e−2. For 0<c′<c, 0<ε<1/3,

\Delta_M^{\rm mic}=d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(I\mid A_M),\mathcal L(P))\ll e^{-c'\nu_M}+M^{-1/3+\varepsilon}.
d_{\rm TV}\!\left(\mathcal L(\Phi_M(I)\mid A_M),\mathcal L(\Phi_M(P))\right)\le\Delta_M^{\rm mic}.

Any common measurable readout or Markov kernel inherits the bound without a partition, dimension or query-count penalty. This comparison excludes the initial run from 1. Each continuum, record or Gaussian limit still needs its stated topology and horizon conditions.

The direct shell theorem, Theorem 2.3, also allows Λ≥1 under its own conditions. Shells remain useful for ordering and censoring; they no longer describe the full spatial scope of the binary input.

02Theorem 2.4 ↗Theorem 3.7 ↗Theorem 5.2 ↗

A Brownian history independent of terminal extremes

Under the growing-history hypotheses, write N_M^{\rm obs} for the number of recognized global record episodes, D_M^{\rm obs} for positive minus negative records, and A_M^{\rm obs}(v)=\int_0^v Z^{\rm obs}(\lfloor m_0 2^s\rfloor+1)\,ds for signed logarithmic leadership. Then

G_M(t):=\frac1{\sqrt T}\begin{pmatrix}N_M^{\rm obs}(Tt)-Tt/2\\D_M^{\rm obs}(Tt)\\A_M^{\rm obs}(Tt)\end{pmatrix}\Rightarrow B_\Sigma(t),\qquad\Sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1/4&0&0\\0&1/2&1\\0&1&\dfrac{\pi^2}{6(\log2)^2}\end{pmatrix}.

Convergence holds in D([0,1],\mathbb R^3) with J_1, without selecting a lattice phase. Counters start at the initial prefix; the inherited maximum and its holder remain in force.

The terminal field, phase and visible maxima

Set b=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor,\ \theta_M=\log_2n-b. Along \theta_M\to\theta, the signed terminal field has limit

\Pi_\theta\sim\operatorname{PPP}\!\left(du\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z}\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}2^{\theta-q-2}\delta_{(q,\sigma)}\right),\quad u\in[0,1].

The field \Xi_M places an atom at (j/n,L+e-b,\sigma) for every retained run starting at j+1 with full length L+e and sign \sigma. For each fixed S>0 and -S\le s\le0, define Y_M(s)=R^{\rm obs}(\lfloor n2^s\rfloor+1)-b and Z_M(s)=Z^{\rm obs}(\lfloor n2^s\rfloor+1). The terminal paths use this fixed interval and this constant integer center.

Let Q_\theta(u) be its maximum mark by position u, and Z_\theta(s) its earliest-holder sign by 2^s. Jointly,

(G_M,\Xi_M,Y_M,Z_M)\Rightarrow(B_\Sigma,\Pi_\theta,Q_\theta(2^\cdot),Z_\theta),\qquad B_\Sigma\ \perp\!\!\!\perp\ (\Pi_\theta,Q_\theta(2^\cdot),Z_\theta).

The terminal field and both paths remain functions of the same cloud. Their topologies are vague convergence, scalar M_1 for the visible maximum and scalar J_1 for its sign. Right censoring makes the maximum fail to be J_1-tight: successive unit improvements can collapse into one limiting jump. This is asymptotic independence, not finite-prefix independence.

03Theorem 6.1 ↗Proposition 6.2 ↗

The observation clock changes the occupation law

Logarithmic positive leadership tends to one half. In physical time, let O_M^+ be the fraction of the first n queried prefixes whose visible holder is positive. Under the same transfer assumptions, along \theta_M\to\theta,

O_M^+\Rightarrow\frac{1+V_\theta}{2},\qquad V_\theta=\int_0^1 Z_\theta(\log_2u)\,du,\qquad\operatorname{Var}(V_\theta)=K(\theta)>0.
K(\theta)=\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z}\frac{1-e^{-2a_q}-2a_qe^{-a_q}}{a_q^2},\quad a_q=2^{\theta-q-1},\qquad\int_0^1K(\theta)\,d\theta=2-\frac1{\log2}.

The physical occupation law remains nondegenerate; its variance is K(\theta)/4. The phase modulation is small but nonzero. Averaging over phase is an identity about this family of laws, not an assumption that the arithmetic phase is random.

04Proposition 7.1 ↗Theorem 7.2 ↗Theorem 7.3 ↗

A canonical interpolation produces a stationary scale flow

Attach independent fractional marks U\in(0,1) with density g(u)=2(\log2)2^{-u}. This preserves every integer threshold while making a geometric excess plus U exponential. In the diffuse planar Poisson target,

X_s(y)=\mathcal P((0,2^s]\times(0,2^{-s-y})),\qquad c_y=2^{-y}.

For fixed y, the count is stationary and reversible with law \operatorname{Pois}(c_y). For h\ge0,

X_{s+h}(y)\mid X_s(y)=m\ \overset d=\ \operatorname{Bin}(m,2^{-h})+\operatorname{Pois}(c_y(1-2^{-h})),

with independent summands. Under the growing-history assumptions, the canonically marked arithmetic paths at any fixed finite family of thresholds couple to this flow on [-T,0], with vector J_1 distance tending to zero in probability.

Auxiliary interpolation is a declared change of observable

The fractional marks are not uniform. They give the exact exponential lifetime needed for the Poisson geometry. Continuously ranked records after interpolation differ from strict integer records; the latter remain the observables in Result 02.

\mathcal L_y\varphi(m)=(\log2)c_y[\varphi(m+1)-\varphi(m)]+(\log2)m[\varphi(m-1)-\varphi(m)].

The generator uses base-two logarithmic time. The interpolation and the target process do not make the finite arithmetic sequence exactly Markovian. The finite retained height and the terminal spatial boundary are respected in the transfer.

05Theorems 7.7–7.13 ↗

The signed spectrum, OU paths and correlated compressions

Under Input 2.1, write \alpha=(e,\sigma),\quad\gamma_\alpha=2^{-e-2},\quad\mu_n=n^{-1}\sum_{j=1}^n\delta_{j/n}. Center by the deterministic Poisson target mean:

Z_{M,\alpha}=\frac{\sum_j I_{j,e,\sigma}\delta_{j/n}-\Lambda\gamma_\alpha\mu_n}{\sqrt\Lambda}.

For s>1/2 and fixed positive weights with \Gamma(w)=\sum_\alpha w_\alpha\gamma_\alpha<\infty,

(Z_{M,\alpha})_\alpha\Rightarrow(\sqrt{\gamma_\alpha}W_\alpha)_\alpha\quad\text{in }\mathcal H_{w,s},\quad\|(F_\alpha)\|^2=\sum_\alpha w_\alpha\|F_\alpha\|_{-s}^2.

The white noises are independent. After canonical interpolation, the standardized sign-resolved survivor paths converge on every fixed threshold horizon to independent spatial OU processes:

H_M^\sigma(t)=\frac{S_M^\sigma(t)-(\Lambda/2)2^{-t}\mu_n}{\sqrt{(\Lambda/2)2^{-t}}},

Here SMσ(t) is the sign-resolved survivor measure. The two paths converge in J₁ on every fixed [0,T₀], with values in (H^{-s})^2.

\operatorname{Cov}(\langle H_t^\sigma,\varphi\rangle,\langle H_u^\tau,\psi\rangle)=\mathbf1_{\sigma=\tau}2^{-|t-u|/2}\int_0^1\varphi\psi.
Two threshold compressions, one correlated limit

Put J=⌊log₂Λ⌋. The discrete and continuous compressions are

D_M^\sigma=J^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^J H_M^\sigma(j)\otimes\delta_{j/J},\quad C_M^\sigma=J^{-1/2}\int_0^J H_M^\sigma(t)\otimes\delta_{t/J}\,dt.

Along a converging lattice phase, their tensor white-noise limit has covariance density

\begin{pmatrix}c_d&c_c\\c_c&c_c\end{pmatrix},\qquad c_d=3+2\sqrt2,\quad c_c=4/\log2.

Thus C^\sigma=\sqrt{c_c}W_c^\sigma,\quad D^\sigma=C^\sigma+\sqrt{c_d-c_c}W_\perp^\sigma with independent noises. The discrete and continuous compressions themselves are highly correlated. The joint spatial limit has three independent blocks: (exact spectrum + fixed-threshold paths); (correlated compressions); (critical Poisson field). The record-history Brownian block is a separate theorem.

Theorems 7.10–7.11 ↗ · Corollary 7.12 ↗
Rectangles, centering and direction-dependent OU rates

For the Gaussian rectangle field,

\operatorname{Cov}(G(a,b),G(c,d))=2^{-(|a-c|+|b-d|)/2}.

A line of direction (p,q) has OU rate (log2)(|p|+|q|)/2: threshold and scale motion therefore give different constants. The signed arithmetic rectangle theorem fixes a compact set K ⊂ (−∞,0] × ℝ, uses kM→∞ with 2^{k_M}\max_K2^{-b}<\Lambda and 2^{k_M}/n^2\to0. The latter controls continuous-area centering against the exact grid mean. A negative-Sobolev field limit does not by itself justify tracing a diagonal path.

Exact definitions, censoring and the two path topologies

For a run with start a_i, full length q_i and previous global maximum H_{i-1}, a strict record has q_i>H_{i-1} and is recognized at

c_i=a_i+H_{i-1},\qquad R^{\rm obs}(m)=\max_i\max\{0,\min(q_i,m-a_i+1)\},\qquad R^{\rm ann}(m)=\max_{a_i\le m}q_i.

The full-length comparison uses final lengths only for runs that have already started. It is an analytical view that knows the continuation. Each maximum keeps the first holder on a tie. Every example is completed by alternating one-symbol runs beyond the displayed word.

In the default example, the final record is recognized at m = 28. Its visible height then rises through 8, 9, 10 and 11, but only one new episode is counted. The holder changes once.

In the theorem’s scaling limit, the observation delay vanishes. Scalar M_1 can accommodate the visible staircase within a completed jump; J_1 cannot discard its intermediate heights. The sign path instead has a separate scalar J_1 result. This finite example explains the distinction; it does not prove convergence or assert a vector topology.

If counters were started at prefix 24 in the default example, the inherited maximum would still be 7 with a negative holder. The positive run begun at 21 would count when recognized at 28. Starting the counters does not reset the history.

Exact laws, time units and the arithmetic interpretation

For 0 ≤ t ≤ S = 3 and a unit-intensity cloud on (0,1] × (0,c2S), define

F_t=\mathcal P((0,2^{t-S}]\times(0,c2^S)),\qquad X_t=\mathcal P((0,2^{t-S}]\times(0,c2^{S-t})).
\mathbb EF_t=c2^t,\quad\mathbb EX_t=c,\qquad\operatorname{Cov}(F_t,F_r)=c2^{\min(t,r)},\quad\operatorname{Cov}(X_t,X_r)=c2^{-|t-r|}.

Every queried rectangle lies within the simulated domain. The scale path starts in its stationary Poisson law. A constant mean does not imply a constant sample path. These counts are not numbers of strict integer records.

The generator in Result 04 uses doublings; changing to natural logarithmic time removes the factor log 2 from both rates. This is a simulation of the exact Poisson target. Arithmetic paths approximate it only under the stated coupling hypotheses, with the prescribed auxiliary marks.

Standardized correlations decay as 2⁻|ᵗ⁻ʳ|/² for the prefix and 2⁻|ᵗ⁻ʳ| for scale. This geometry explains the two Gaussian correlation rates; a finite-intensity Poisson path is not an OU sample.

03 · Exact Poisson-target variance

Leadership variance: a small but nonzero phase modulation

For the signed Poisson record field, let V_\theta=\int_0^1Z_\theta(\log_2u)\,du be the signed occupation imbalance. The earliest holder keeps a tied record. Its variance is

K(\theta)=\sum_{q\in\mathbb Z}\frac{1-e^{-2a_q}-2a_qe^{-a_q}}{a_q^2},\qquad a_q=2^{\theta-q-1},\qquad\overline K=2-\frac1{\log2}.

Observable, transfer hypotheses and numerical evaluation

The signed field on [0,1]\times\mathbb Z\times\{\pm1\} has intensity du\sum_{r,\sigma}2^{\theta-r-2}\delta_{(r,\sigma)}. The sign Z_\theta(\log_2u) belongs to the earliest holder of the highest mark seen by physical time u. The positive leadership fraction is O_\theta^+=(1+V_\theta)/2, hence its variance is K(\theta)/4.

For the arithmetic observation size, the phase is \theta_M=\{\log_2 n\},\quad n=M-L. The limiting occupation law is taken along a sequence with this phase converging to θ.

The series is an exact Poisson-target formula, 1-periodic in θ. Transfer to the arithmetic model uses the hypotheses of Records and scale flows Theorems 2.3, 5.2 and 6.1; it is not an exact finite-prefix variance formula. Small arguments are evaluated by a power series to avoid cancellation; the dyadic sum uses indices −32 to 52 with compensated summation.

Model, normalization and exact finite covariance

Take μ=2^(J+θ−1), a Poisson(μ) population and independent exponential lifetimes with rate log2. If N(t) is the survivor count, H(t)=(N(t)−μ2⁻ᵗ)/√(μ2⁻ᵗ). We compute C by an exact integral of those lifetimes, without quadrature.

D_J=J^{-1/2}\sum_{j=0}^J H(j),\quad C_J=J^{-1/2}\int_0^J H(t)\,dt.

With α=(log2)/2 and r=e⁻ᵅ, the covariance kernel is e^(−α|t−u|), so

v_D=\frac{J+1+2\sum_{k=1}^J(J+1-k)r^k}{J},\quad v_C=\frac2\alpha-\frac{2(1-r^J)}{J\alpha^2},
c_{DC}=\frac2{J\alpha}\left(J+1-\sum_{j=0}^Jr^j\right).

This samples the Poisson target, not the arithmetic function. Second moments are computed directly for that target. In the joint Gaussian limit, C and D−C become independent; finite J retains a correlation.
