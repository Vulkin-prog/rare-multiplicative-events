Poisson laws and arithmetic clusters for rare multiplicative patterns

Brice Pouly · Independent author · ORCID · Public edition V1

On this page. Prime-source corrections and fixed-environment cluster laws are the central results. Rational probes, long Borel patterns and deterministic packets are separate extensions.

The prelimit kernel is uniform in exceptional phases. Their moving-scale classification belongs to Moving prime environments.

Central resultsExtensionsPrime sourceClustersErasureResolutionConnectionsSources

Completely multiplicative circle-valued functions can have independent nonuniform prime values, or share a fixed finite exceptional prime environment.

Rare prime sites can carry a disproportionate amount of target mass; exact relations inside an exceptional environment can turn one random source into several occurrences.

The paper preserves the true prime-source intensities and compares the field to a germ kernel, then classifies corrected counts through the actual integer relation lattice.

Two central results

Prime sources and fixed environments first; observation extensions follow.

01Theorem A · p. 3 ↗

Retain the rare-event mass carried by primes

Let m be Haar probability on the circle. Assume independent prime densities g_p\ge0,\ \int g_p\,dm=1,\ \sup_p\int g_p^2\,dm\le K_2<\infty. Fix C\ge1,A>0, deterministic masks S_N\subset\{2,\ldots,\lfloor CN\rfloor\} and Borel targets J_{N,n} with masses a_n=m(J_{N,n})\le A/N. For \Xi_N=\sum_{n\in S_N}\mathbf1_{\{F(n)\in J_{N,n}\}}\delta_{(n,F(n))}, set

\mu_N=\sum_{\substack{n\in S_N\\n\ \mathrm{composite}}}\delta_n\otimes\mathbf1_{J_{N,n}}m+\sum_{\substack{p\in S_N\\p\ \mathrm{prime}}}\delta_p\otimes\mathbf1_{J_{N,p}}g_pm.

For Y=(\log N)^4 and \mathcal F_Y=\sigma(F(p):p\le Y), some \kappa=\kappa(K_2)>0 gives

\mathbb E\,d_{\rm TV}\!\left(\mathcal L(\Xi_N\mid\mathcal F_Y),\operatorname{PPP}(\mu_N)\right)\ll_{C,A,K_2}(\log N)^{-\kappa}.

The count keeps the exact moving parameter \lambda_N=\Lambda_N-A_N^{\rm p}+Q_N, with \Lambda_N=\sum_na_n, A_N^{\rm p}=\sum_pa_p and Q_N=\sum_p\int_{J_{N,p}}g_p\,dm. All sums are restricted to the chosen mask, and the prime sums to its prime sites. The prime contribution may diverge. The entirely Haar target is recovered exactly when Q_N\to0.

Uniformity, the L² threshold and an explicit rate

The estimate is uniform over the masks, targets and densities above. Set \rho=1-(16K_2^2)^{-1} and I(\vartheta)=1-\vartheta+\vartheta\log\vartheta. Theorem 3.1 permits 0<\kappa<\min\{I(\vartheta),-\vartheta\log\rho\},\quad 0<\vartheta<1. The parameter K_2 bounds the squared norm. Proposition 3.5 makes the exponent 2 sharp in the uniform L^q scale for this target and arbitrary masks; it does not say that every non-L² density prevents every Poisson law.

02Theorem B · p. 5 ↗

The integer relation lattice determines the cluster law

Fix a finite exceptional prime set \mathcal E and phases \xi\in\mathbb T^{\mathcal E}. All other prime values are independent Haar. Let \mathcal D_{\mathcal E} be the positive integers supported on \mathcal E, \chi_\xi(s)=\prod_{p\in\mathcal E}\xi_p^{v_p(s)} and \delta_{\mathcal E}=\prod_{p\in\mathcal E}(1-p^{-1}). Use the arc about 1 of mass \tau/N.

L_\xi=\{v\in\mathbb Z^{\mathcal E}:\xi^v=1\},\qquad B_N(\xi)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal D_{\mathcal E}\cap[2,N]}\mathbf1_{\{\chi_\xi(s)\in J_N\}}.

Partition the multipliers into equal-phase classes \mathcal A. Within each class list s_{\mathcal A,1}<s_{\mathcal A,2}<\cdots, using 1/\infty=0 for a finite list. Define

\lambda_j(\xi)=\tau\delta_{\mathcal E}\sum_{\mathcal A}\left(\frac1{s_{\mathcal A,j}}-\frac1{s_{\mathcal A,j+1}}\right).
\sum_{n=2}^N\mathbf1_{\{F(n)\in J_N\}}-B_N(\xi)\xrightarrow{d_{\rm TV}}\sum_{j\ge1}jP_j,\qquad P_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_j(\xi))\ \text{independently}.

The jump intensities satisfy \sum_jj\lambda_j=\tau. The limit is \operatorname{Pois}(\tau) if and only if L_\xi=\{0\}.

Exact correction, fixed environment and the prelimit kernel

B_N(\xi) is an exactly known configuration count, not an expectation to subtract. The lattice is the full, possibly nonsaturated integer subgroup; its quotient may have torsion. Thus its rational span or a floating-point phase comparison is insufficient. Fix \xi before taking N\to\infty. The classification gives no uniform rate for moving near-relations.

Theorem 4.1 allows independent L²-bounded densities outside an arbitrary fixed finite environment, independent of the regular prime source, with a conditional target of phase germs. Writing n=sd, each germ carries all activated sites sd. Prime cores retain their true density, even at composite sites. The d=1 configuration remains exact.

03Theorem 8.4 · p. 37 ↗

Long Borel patterns pay for the constraints that are erased

Let K=H\times\mathbb T^d be fixed, with H finite abelian and independent Haar prime values of law \mu. Take \beta_-\log N\le B\le\beta_+\log N and a Borel event A_N\subset K^B of independent mass a>0. The mark records the entire window. Define

D_r(A)=\frac1a\max_{\substack{J\subset\{0,\ldots,B-1\}\\|J|=B-r}}(\mu^{\otimes J})^*(\operatorname{pr}_J A),\qquad D_r(A_N)\le C_0z^r,

with fixed C_0\ge1,z>1. Let o_s(A) be the independent probability of two copies at shift s, and set

\Lambda=Na,\qquad\Omega(A)=a^{-1}\sum_{s=1}^{B-1}o_s(A),\qquad\mathfrak m=\max\{1,az^B\}.

If \Lambda_N stays in a compact subset of (0,\infty), \mathfrak m_N\le N^{1/2-\eta} for fixed 0<\eta<1/2 and \Omega(A_N)\to0, then, for 0<c<1/\sqrt2,

\mathbb E\,d_{\rm TV}\!\left(\mathcal L(\Xi_N\mid\mathcal F_Y),\operatorname{PPP}\!\left(\sum_{x\in I_N}\delta_x\otimes\mathbf1_A\mu^{\otimes B}\right)\right)=O\!\left(\Omega(A_N)+e^{-c\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}}+N^{-\eta/2}\right),

where I_N=[N,2N)\cap\mathbb Z, \Xi_N=\sum_{x\in I_N}\mathbf1_{\{(F(x-1),\ldots,F(x+B-2))\in A\}}\delta_{(x,(F(x-1),\ldots,F(x+B-2)))} and Y=\exp(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}/\sqrt2).

How this extends Long runs, and the general error budget
\mathbb E d_{\rm TV}\ll\Lambda\Omega(A)+\Lambda(1+\Lambda)e^{-c\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}}+N^{-1/3+\varepsilon}\left(\Lambda+\Lambda^{11/6}\mathfrak m^{1/6}+\Lambda^{4/3}\mathfrak m^{2/3}\right).

Here 0<\varepsilon<1/3. The binary relation profile is imported from Long runs V3, with variable weight base z and an additional integer-minor argument. Changing the weight base does not change the characteristic of that relation space. For an h-letter dictionary W, take z=h,\ \mathfrak m=|W|. Binary words recover the normalization of Long runs.

The outer measure in D_r matters for general Borel projections. The actual event is retained, not only its equivalence class modulo Haar-null sets. Erasure control and local overlap control are separate requirements.

04Proposition 10.4 · p. 45 ↗

Observation resolution has a finite-source obstruction

Suppose V\in\mathbb N_0^m is a deterministic function of a random origin with at most N_{\rm orig} possible values. Set \lambda=\sum_c\lambda_c and \lambda_{\max}=\max_c\lambda_c\le1. For every integer k\ge1,

d_{\rm TV}\!\left(\mathcal L(V),\bigotimes_{c=1}^m\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_c)\right)\ge\mathbb P(\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda)\ge k)-N_{\rm orig}e^{-\lambda}\lambda_{\max}^{\,k}.

A scalar count may be close to Poisson while a detailed position field is not. Proposition 10.3 gives a complementary upper bound using signed factorial packets at the chosen spatial partition; their norm contracts when cells are merged.

The boundary for deterministic signs, including Liouville

The packet identities hold for every deterministic sign sequence and every stated law of the origin. They do not supply the remaining correlation estimates for Liouville. Fixing all prime signs to −1 is not a permitted density change from the random model. Nor does a good count histogram establish microscopic field convergence.

For N_{\rm orig}=2^{20},\ H=2^{14},\ \lambda=1,\ \lambda_{\max}=1/H, choosing k=2 yields 1-(2+1/256)e^{-1}=0.262804\ldots. This obstruction is independent of the chosen deterministic signs.

A second route to rarity: rational observations

A product or ratio can enter one very small target without its individual factors being constrained. That situation uses private prime witnesses and equality/inversion classes, rather than the long-pattern erasure argument.

Theorem 6.1 · Haar prime values and the resonance deficit

On I_N, let R_N(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{k_N}(x+s_{N,j})^{e_{N,j}}\ne1 with distinct 0\le s_{N,1}<\cdots<s_{N,k_N}\le CN and nonzero integer exponents. The data may move with N. For a Borel target J of mass a, write R_N(x)=r_{\mathcal C}^{\sigma_x},\ r_{\mathcal C}>1,\ \sigma_x\in\{\pm1\} within each equality/inversion class and put

\Lambda=Na,\quad p_{\mathcal C}=m\!\left(\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal C}J^{\sigma_x}\right),\quad\mathfrak d=\Lambda-\sum_{\mathcal C}p_{\mathcal C},\quad\epsilon_N^*=2a|D^*|+4Na^2+16a^2E_Y.

The sets D^{\rm ph},D^{\rm full} are the certificate deletion sets in (6.5)–(6.6), and E_Y counts ordered large-prime graph edges. For full sampled-value marks, the simple Haar target has mean conditional TV error at most \epsilon_N^{\rm full}+2\mathfrak d. Conversely,

d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(Z_N),\operatorname{Pois}(\Lambda))\ge e^{-\Lambda}(e^{\mathfrak d}-1)-\epsilon_N^{\rm ph}.

With bounded \Lambda_N and \epsilon_N^{\rm full}\to0, simple Poisson convergence is equivalent to \mathfrak d_N\to0. A sufficient full-mark support budget is \log k_N=o(\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}). For example, R_N(x)=x/(x+h_N),\ 1\le h_N\le CN has no equal or inverted pairs, so its deficit is zero even for a macroscopic offset.

Theorem 7.2 · Permanent prime bias can be handled locally

The rational branch also treats independent densities 0\le g_p\le K under a smaller support budget. For a suitable \kappa>0, its phase-field error from the Haar cluster target is O((\Lambda_N+\Lambda_N^2)k_N(\log N)^{-\kappa}) at bounded intensity when k_N\le(\log N)^\kappa. The simple full-value target requires the additional term 3\mathfrak d_N. With k_N=o((\log N)^\kappa) and bounded intensity, the vanishing resonance deficit again characterizes the simple target.

For g(e^{it})=1+\tfrac12\cos t, the two-value ratio example admits the rate O((\log N)^{-1/20}). The infinite prime-source law can be singular to Haar; this is a local reconstruction, not an unrestricted global change of measure.

Exact calculation and asymptotic interpretation

A phase is a position on a circle; Haar means uniform around that circle. In angular turns u, let ℓ(u) = log(1/(2|u|)) and gᵣ(u) = ℓ(u)ʳ/r!, for r = 0, 1, 2. These are probability densities with finite L² norms.

a=\tau/N,\quad q_r(a)=a\sum_{j=0}^r\frac{\log^j(1/a)}{j!},\quad\lambda_N=(N-P_N)a+P_Nq_r(a)

P_N is the exact number of primes in the interval. For r = 1, the prime intensity tends to τ and the total target mean to 2τ. For r = 2, the prime contribution grows like (τ/2) log N. The displayed target is not the exact finite-N law of the multiplicative count. No finite-N TV error is certified by this plot.

What is exact here, and what is a limiting statement?

The rows show multiplicative identities in a finite prefix, not independent random draws. U has infinite order; in the F(2) = ±1 examples F(3) is independent Haar, hence almost surely of infinite order, and the exceptional set is {2}. The no-relation preset assumes 1 and the two angular turns are rationally independent. No relation is inferred numerically. All other prime values are independent Haar.

The probability chart is the theoretical limiting law of the full corrected prefix count. In the F(2) = ±1 presets, the regular cores in the rows are 5 × 3ᵇ; in the other presets the exceptional set is {2,3} and the core is 5. In the rank-one cases, set A = 3, 2, 4 respectively and p = 1 − 1/A.

W=\sum_{i=1}^{P}G_i,\quad P\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\tau p),\quad\mathbb P(G_i=j)=pA^{-(j-1)}

The germs and their positive geometric sizes are independent in this limiting representation. With F(2) = F(3) = U and U Haar, every individual F(n) is Haar even though the limiting count is compound Poisson. After averaging U, the exceptional-only correction vanishes in probability. For fixed environments the correction must remain explicit.

The fixed word, exact enumeration and signed packets

This word was selected to make the contrast visible. It is a deliberately chosen deterministic example, not Liouville and not a random multiplicative sample.

All 64 origins are enumerated. The target has independent Poisson cell counts of mean 1/m, hence total mean 1 at every resolution. Actual total mean: 15/16. The total histogram is (24,24,12,4)/64 for counts 0,1,2,3.

d_{\rm TV}=1-\sum_{v\in S}\min\left\{\mathbb P(V=v),e^{-1}\prod_c\frac{(1/m)^{v_c}}{v_c!}\right\}

S is the finite set of observed count vectors. This overlap formula includes the Poisson mass outside S exactly, without tail truncation. The distance is the largest probability gap for a question about the counts, not a fraction of incorrectly located points. A zero support bound is inconclusive.

The second packet sums only compatible ordered start pairs, |j−k| ≥ 4, subtracting 1/256 before aggregating signed terms by cell pair and taking absolute values. It is an input to an upper bound with additional overlap and tail terms, not a TV lower bound or a proof of asymptotic cancellation.

Where to go next

From fixed to moving phases

Arithmetic clusters’ prelimit germ kernel is uniform over phases of a fixed finite exceptional prime set. Its limiting count classification fixes those phases. Moving prime environments keeps the set fixed but allows the phases to vary with N, resolves near-relations at target width 1/N and identifies spatial cluster laws.
