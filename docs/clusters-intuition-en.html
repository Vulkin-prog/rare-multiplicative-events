From Long runs: the two sources of a long run

Guided reading · Arithmetic clusters

Same chance does not mean separate arrivals.

Imagine six lamps. Each lights up about one time in six. If some share a switch, several can light up together. Their individual chances can stay exactly the same, while the number of lit lamps behaves very differently.

That distinction is central to Arithmetic clusters. We will connect it to multiplicative numbers, then ask what happens when rare events come from a small population—or when we forget part of their positions.

This page studies shared causes and the sources of rare events, with fixed settings at exceptional primes. Moving environments have their own page.

01 · Shared causes

Numbers can share an invisible switch.

Long runs used two values, +1 and −1, chosen by independent fair coin tosses at primes. Here a value may instead be an angle around a circle, like a hand on a clock. Multiplying two circle values adds their angles.

Give 2 and 3 the same value U. Then 10 = 2 × 5 and 15 = 3 × 5 have the same value too. Likewise, 20, 30 and 45 share U²F(5). If we look for values inside one small arc, the members of each group enter it together.

What the theorem adds

On the continuous circle, the paper proves a limit law for the whole prefix of integers. Poisson describes the number of groups; a second law describes each group’s size. This is a compound Poisson law. Exact multiplicative relations determine its parameters.

Explore exact phase classes and their limiting law

Some known sites may always hit: if F(2) = 1, all powers of 2 equal 1. The theorem keeps that known contribution separately. It does not replace it by an average.

02 · A small population can matter

Few people in a queue can still generate half the calls.

Imagine a service with many occasional callers and a small group who call much more often. Counting people alone would underestimate that group’s share of the calls. The paper gives an arithmetic version of this effect.

Prime numbers become less frequent among large integers. But the model may give each prime a higher chance of hitting the small arc. These two effects can balance: fewer prime sites, yet a contribution that does not disappear.

With τ = 1 and the logarithmic cusp, increase N. The prime fraction falls, while the corrected mean approaches 2 instead of 1. The “cusp” is just a probability density concentrated near the target.

Exact calculation and asymptotic interpretation

A phase is a position on a circle; Haar means uniform around that circle. In angular turns u, let ℓ(u) = log(1/(2|u|)) and gᵣ(u) = ℓ(u)ʳ/r!, for r = 0, 1, 2. These are probability densities with finite L² norms.

a=\tau/N,\quad q_r(a)=a\sum_{j=0}^r\frac{\log^j(1/a)}{j!},\quad\lambda_N=(N-P_N)a+P_Nq_r(a)

P_N is the exact number of primes in the interval. For r = 1, the prime intensity tends to τ and the total target mean to 2τ. For r = 2, the prime contribution grows like (τ/2) log N. 03 · What an event can detect

Eight separate checks, or one final check?

Suppose an access rule asks eight independent yes/no questions and requires every answer to be yes. Each answer has probability 1/2. Passing all eight is rare: one chance in 256. Forget one answer, and seven conditions still remain.

Now use one check on the product of eight circle values, with the same overall probability. If one value is missing, it can always be chosen to make the product pass. One forgotten coordinate removes the entire restriction on the others.

03 · Exact projection calculation

Same rarity. Very different cost of forgetting.

Compare two events of the same probability for B independent uniform circle values: every coordinate lies in an arc of mass p, or their product lies in one arc of mass pᴮ. Erasing a coordinate means asking whether some missing value could complete the event.

Try B = 8, p = 1/2, r = 1: forgetting one value multiplies the allowed mass by 2 for separate constraints, and by 256 for the product. One vertical unit on the log₂ chart means a doubling of this cost.

Projection cost and the connection to Long runs

D_r^{\rm coord}=p^{-r},\qquad D_0^{\rm prod}=1,\quad D_r^{\rm prod}=p^{-B}\quad(r\ge1)

These are exact projection masses in the independent reference model. Erasure is projection, not conditioning a deleted value to 1. A single free circle value can complete any product target, which explains the jump. The coordinate event has envelope z = 1/p and effective size 1. This alone does not establish Poisson convergence: local overlaps must also be controlled. Counting run starts with a left mismatch handles that different issue.

Definition 8.1 · §9.6 · Long runs: the other condition, local overlaps

This is the direct bridge to Long runs. A long pattern accumulates many constraints; Arithmetic clusters measures the price of losing some of them and extends the method to more colours and more general target sets. Nearby overlaps still need a separate check.

For a product or ratio constraint, the paper supplies a different method. One example is near-alignment of two clock hands: F(x)/F(x+h) enters a small arc around 1, even when h is comparable to N. See this other source of rarity.

04 · Choose the right level of detail

“How many?” is a different question from “where?”

Two weather models can predict the same number of showers and disagree about which neighbourhoods get wet. Likewise, matching the total number of rare patterns does not mean their positions are correctly modelled.

Start with one cell: only the total is kept. Then split the observation into 2, 4, 8 and 16 cells. The total histogram stays the same, but the position information reveals a larger mismatch.

The fixed word, exact enumeration and signed packets

This word was selected to make the contrast visible. It is a deliberately chosen deterministic example, not Liouville and not a random multiplicative sample.

All 64 origins are enumerated. The target has independent Poisson cell counts of mean 1/m, hence total mean 1 at every resolution. Actual total mean: 15/16. The total histogram is (24,24,12,4)/64 for counts 0,1,2,3.

d_{\rm TV}=1-\sum_{v\in S}\min\left\{\mathbb P(V=v),e^{-1}\prod_c\frac{(1/m)^{v_c}}{v_c!}\right\}

S is the finite set of observed count vectors. This overlap formula includes the Poisson mass outside S exactly, without tail truncation. The distance is the largest probability gap for a question about the counts, not a fraction of incorrectly located points. A zero support bound is inconclusive.

The second packet sums only compatible ordered start pairs, |j−k| ≥ 4, subtracting 1/256 before aggregating signed terms by cell pair and taking absolute values. It is an input to an upper bound with additional overlap and tail terms, not a TV lower bound or a proof of asymptotic cancellation.

When the settings move closer

If the gap between two values shrinks as fast as the target, they can keep triggering hits together. The exceptional primes stay the same: their angles move.
