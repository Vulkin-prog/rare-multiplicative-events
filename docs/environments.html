Moving prime environments and arithmetic cluster limits

Brice Pouly · Public edition V1

On this page. The exceptional prime set is finite and fixed; its phases may vary arbitrarily with N. Independent Haar values at all other primes supply the random source. The results classify corrected cluster fields and their spatial observations.

For general source densities and the fixed-phase classification, start with Arithmetic clusters.

ModelFour resultsMoving phasesWindow geometryMethodSources

Let E be a fixed finite set of primes, prescribe F_N(p)=\xi_{N,p}\in\mathbb T on E, and choose independent Haar values at every other prime before extending completely multiplicatively.

A phase error of order 1/N can send one source into several copies of a target of mass τ/N, even when those copies lie far apart.

A direct comparison with a moving Poisson germ kernel, followed by effective integer-subgroup profiles and interval geometry, identifies the resulting cluster laws.

Corrected field and target-scale notation
\mathcal D_E=\{s\ge1:p\mid s\Rightarrow p\in E\},\quad I=(-\tau/2,\tau/2),\quad J_N=e^{2\pi i I/N},
\Xi_N=\sum_{\substack{2\le n\le N\\n\notin\mathcal D_E}}\mathbf1_{\{F_N(n)\in J_N\}}\delta_{(n/N,\,N\operatorname{Arg}(F_N(n))/(2\pi))}.

Remove the exact environment-only configuration, not its expectation. For example, a pinned phase FN(2)=1 gives ⌊log₂N⌋ uncorrected hits at powers of two. The corrected total WN has mean τ(1−BE(N)/N)→τ, where BE(N)=|DE∩[1,N]|.

Four results

01Theorem 3.1 ↗

Target-scale pair overlap characterizes Poisson convergence

For v\in\mathbb Z^E\setminus\{0\}, set A(v)=\prod p^{(v_p)_+},\quad B(v)=\prod p^{(-v_p)_+},\quad H(v)=\max\{A(v),B(v)\} and

r_N(v)=\frac{N}{2\pi}\operatorname{Arg}_0(\xi_N^v),\quad\beta_N(v)=(\tau-|r_N(v)|)_+,\quad R_N=\sum_{v\ne0}\frac{\beta_N(v)}{H(v)}.

Both orientations occur in this absolutely convergent sum. For deterministic phase sequences,

d_{\rm TV}(\mathcal L(W_N),\operatorname{Pois}(\tau))\to0\iff R_N\to0\iff\forall v\ne0,\ \liminf_N N\|\xi_N^v\|_{\mathbb T}\ge\tau.

The equivalent field comparison is to the Poisson process of its exact mean measure on the same grid. Direct second-moment calculation also gives

\operatorname{Var}(W_N)-\mathbb E W_N=R_N+O_{E,\tau}(N^{-1/2}(1+\log N)^{2|E|+2}).

This is not an inference from total variation to unbounded moments, nor a general two-moment Poisson criterion.

02Proposition 5.2 · Theorem 5.4 ↗

Effective integer profiles classify all marked subsequential limits

Every phase sequence has a subsequence with a subgroup L\le\mathbb Z^E and additive map h:L\to\mathbb R such that

r_N(v)\to h(v)\ (v\in L),\qquad |r_N(v)|\to\infty\ (v\notin L).

A subgroup is realizable exactly when \operatorname{Tor}(\mathbb Z^E/L) is cyclic, including the trivial group. The actual integer subgroup is retained: saturation can destroy information.

Group smooth multipliers into classes s\sim_Lt\iff v(s)-v(t)\in L. For a least representative sC, let as=h(v(s)−v(sC)). Independent germs of intensity \delta_E\,dx\,dz,\quad\delta_E=\prod_{p\in E}(1-p^{-1}) on their active domains produce

G_C(x,z)=\sum_{\substack{s\in C\\sx\le1}}\mathbf1_I(z+a_s)\,\delta_{(sx,z+a_s)}.

The marked field converges weakly to this cluster field; all marked subsequential limits arise this way. Counts converge in total variation to

W_{L,h}=\sum_{j\ge1}jP_j,\quad P_j\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\lambda_j)\text{ independently},\quad\sum_jj\lambda_j=\tau.

The prefix path at one terminal N converges in J₁. It is a spatial prefix path, with shared-germ dependence; it is not the stationary scale flow of Records and scale flows.

03Theorems 8.4–8.6 ↗

The complete spatial covariance identifies the profile law

For deterministic profiles, fixed E,τ and the interval target, write \beta(v)=\begin{cases}(\tau-|h(v)|)_+,&v\in L,\\0,&v\notin L.\end{cases}. The unmarked spatial law is determined by its complete second factorial cumulant measure:

K_2^{\rm sp}=\sum_{v\ne0}\beta(v)\int_0^{1/H(v)}\delta_{(B(v)x,A(v)x)}\,dx.
\mathcal L(X_{L,h})=\mathcal L(X_{L',h'})\iff K_2^{\rm sp}=K_2^{\prime,\rm sp}\iff\beta(v)=\beta'(v)\quad(v\ne0).

Each rational ray reveals its overlap coefficient. Full-sequence spatial convergence is equivalent to convergence of every βN(v). A scalar variance, a finite covariance matrix or a total-count law does not identify this field. Environmental mixtures are outside this deterministic identification statement.

What retaining angular marks adds

For a fixed known bounded Borel target, complete covariance on positions and angular marks identifies the marked deterministic-profile law. For intervals, marked convergence requires every pair (\beta_N(v),r_N(v)\beta_N(v)) to converge. Alternating displacements +c and −c can preserve the spatial law while preventing marked convergence.

04Theorems 10.1–10.3 ↗

A spatial window imposes angular and logarithmic constraints

For an interior window U=(a,b),\quad0<a<b\le1, put w=\log(b/a),\quad\ell(v)=\log(A(v)/B(v)). Its restricted marked field is Poisson, equivalently its count is Pois(τ(b−a)), exactly when

\nexists v\in L\setminus\{0\}:\quad |\ell(v)|<w\quad\text{and}\quad |h(v)|<\tau.

The essential maximum number of descendants of one germ is

M_U=\sup\{|V|:V\subset L\text{ finite},\ \operatorname{span}_V\ell<w,\ \operatorname{span}_Vh<\tau\}.

It is finite exactly when \Gamma_{L,h}=\{(\ell(v),h(v)):v\in L\}\subset\mathbb R^2 is discrete. Rank one is locally bounded; rank two is bounded exactly when the two forms are independent; rank at least three is unbounded in every nonempty interior interval.

This bounds descendants of a single germ, not the total number of occurrences.

A tiny rotation, seen through a shrinking target

At size N, the prescribed rotation is c/N turns and the target occupies 1/N of the circle. Magnifying both by N turns the rotation into a step c against a target of width 1. Same full total. Different maps.

Compare rank-one relation channels 3/1 and 3/2, with the same angular displacement d. Their full-prefix count laws are identical. In a crop U=(a,1), their linked positions need not fit together.

From arithmetic sources to visible clusters

01Separate the core

Factor n=sd into an exceptional smooth multiplier and a regular core. Remove d=1 exactly.

02Compare Haar sources

Condition on small regular primes. Haar characters and a weighted core budget control the dependency graph.

03Keep descendants

Effective integer profiles determine which shifted target intervals intersect.

04Reconstruct geometry

Positive forest identities reconstruct interval unions; summable multiplier tails recover the complete law.

The direct Haar-to-germ error is uniform in the exceptional phases:

\varepsilon_H(N)\le\exp\!\left[-\left(1/\sqrt2-o(1)\right)\sqrt{\log N\log\log N}\right].

The Poisson comparison still pays for overlaps RN. This asymptotic rate is not a computable finite-size error bar and does not assert a rate to an arbitrary fixed profile limit.
